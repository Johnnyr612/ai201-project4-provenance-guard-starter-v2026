## Signals and Scoring

<!-- For each signal: what property it measures, why that might differ between
     human and AI writing, and WHAT IT CAN'T SEE.

     The blind spot is not optional. If you can't name it you don't understand
     the signal yet — and unit 8's attack set is built out of blind spots. -->

### Signal one — the model signal

**What it measures:** How predictable my text is to the local GPT-2 model.
Lower perplexity maps to a higher 0–1 score, which means “more AI-like” to
this signal.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** Generated writing
often uses likely next words and can be predictable to another language model.

**What it can't see:** Predictability does not establish authorship. Plain,
ordinary human writing can also be predictable, while unusual human writing
can score as less AI-like.

**Three checks before I wired it into `/submit`:**

| Input description | Perplexity | `model_signal` |
|---|---:|---:|
| AI-authored: “The implementation of the proposed framework requires careful consideration of several key factors. First, it is important to note that the system must handle a variety of inputs. Additionally, performance depends on several important variables.” | 21.6 | 0.7633 |
| My own question: “What does milestone 1 want me to do? Ok so walk me through how you would complete milestone1 and explain your answer for each.” | 73.2 | 0.3146 |
| AI-authored borderline candidate: “I think this plan could work, although I am not sure about the timeline. We should test a small version first and see what causes problems.” | 29.9 | 0.6577 |

The second input is text I wrote in this conversation. The first and third
were generated by the AI assistant for this check, so the third is not a
human-authored example. Its score is close to the starter AI threshold of
0.65, but the label is not implemented yet. These scores are examples of what
this signal returned, not proof of who wrote any text.

### Signal two — the style signal

**What it measures:** Sentence-length spread (sample standard deviation
divided by mean sentence length), type-token ratio (unique words divided by
total words), and punctuation marks per word. The style score normalizes these
to 0–1 and combines them: 45% low sentence variation, 45% low vocabulary
variety, and 10% low punctuation density. Higher means more AI-like.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** Generated writing can
have more even sentence lengths, repeat common vocabulary, and use
conventional punctuation. These are only rough tendencies, so I give
punctuation a smaller weight.

**What it can't see:** It cannot understand meaning or determine authorship.
Short texts and edits that change sentence breaks, word repetition, or
punctuation can move the score without changing who wrote the text. Type-token
ratio also tends to decrease as text gets longer.

### The combining rule

<!-- Write the rule in words, then give the numbers. What happens when the two
     signals disagree? -->

**The rule:** Take the weighted average of the model and style scores. When
they strongly disagree, their average moves toward 0.5 rather than letting
either signal decide by itself. This represents uncertainty; it does not make
the disagreement disappear.

**The numbers:** `0.50 × model_score + 0.50 × style_score`.

**Where it lives:** `scoring.py::combine_signals`

<!-- ⚠️ The grader checks your code against that line. If your rule lives
     somewhere else, say where. -->

**A case where my two signals split, and what my rule does with it:** On the
generic AI explanatory sample below, `model_score` was `0.7633` and
`style_score` was `0.3016`. Their equal-weight average was `0.5324`, near the
middle rather than a high-AI score. I prefer an unsure result to letting one
signal overrule the other, though this rule can also let AI text through.

**Calibration checks:** I used the same five texts for both signals. The
“borderline-style” and “lightly edited AI” rows are AI-generated candidates,
not human-authored borderline cases. The formal human-authored text is an
excerpt from the course brief; the conversational sample is my own prompt.

| Input | `model_score` | `style_score` | Combined |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Clearly AI: generic explanatory paragraph | 0.7633 | 0.3016 | 0.5324 |
| Human-authored: my conversational Milestone 4 prompt | 0.3847 | 0.3908 | 0.3877 |
| Borderline-style candidate: AI-generated | 0.6577 | 0.4503 | 0.5540 |
| Formal human-authored: course-brief excerpt | 0.2460 | 0.1506 | 0.1983 |
| Lightly edited AI candidate: AI-generated | 0.6074 | 0.4366 | 0.5220 |

This is a small calibration set, not evidence that the scores identify
authorship reliably. For this milestone, `confidence` is
`2 × abs(combined_score - 0.5)`: distance from the midpoint, not a calibrated
probability. The `guess` and reader-facing label remain provisional until
Milestone 5.
